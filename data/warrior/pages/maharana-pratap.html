Maharana Pratap

1540 – 1597 | Mewar Dynasty

The symbol of unyielding Rajput courage, self-respect, and eternal resistance against imperial expansion.

The Kingdom of Mewar

Maharana Pratap was the 13th king of Mewar, a region in north-western India. Unlike many contemporary rulers who capitulated to the Mughal Empire, Pratap rejected sovereignty under Emperor Akbar, choosing decades of hardship over submission.

His reign is defined by guerrilla tactics, legendary patriotism, and his iconic war horse, Chetak, who gave his life during battle to save his master.

Greatest Battles & Campaigns

Battle of Haldighati

1576 AD

Outcome: Strategic Stalemate & Continued Resistance

Though outnumbered, Pratap’s forces inflicted heavy losses on the Mughal army. The victory was imperial on paper, but Akbar failed to capture Pratap or bend Mewar's resolve.

Battle of Dewair

1582 AD

Outcome: Decisive Rajput Victory

Known as the "Marathon of Mewar," Pratap launched a massive counter-offensive, destroying the Mughal outposts in Mewar and recapturing majority of his lost territories.

Liberation of Mewar

1585 – 1597 AD

Outcome: Full Recovery & Capital at Chavand

Taking advantage of Akbar’s focus on the Northwest frontier, Pratap reclaimed almost all of Mewar (except Chittorgarh) and established a prosperous new capital at Chavand.

Legacy & Heritage

Maharana Pratap remains an enduring symbol of liberty, military innovation, and moral fortitude. His battle strategies heavily influenced later Indian rulers, including Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, in adopting effective guerrilla tactics against larger empires.
